# #243, M9's desktop Stage 1 probe: one export and three arms, the filter and its clock, what the corpus expresses, and what the seeds leave readable (2026-11-10)

**A Stage 1 probe under [the step protocol](./step-protocol.md), run on 2026-11-10: headless desktop
replays of the campaign's clips, RANSAC seeded per frame, seeds 1 to 8, policy `frame`. Not campaign
data; never reportable as a round. It registers nothing: no criterion, prediction or threshold, no
Stage 2 or Stage 3 text, no change under `packages/*/src`, `examples/` or `scripts/`, no change to
`METRICS_VERSION`. The registration (M9's step 4) is written from this record, by another session.
The tilt clips' device acquisition is borrowed from the static clip (36.9 ms), not measured, and every
figure on those schedules is labelled.**

## The method

- **Clips:** `pinball-static.mp4`, `pinball-bench-table.mp4`, `pinball-bench.mp4` (the wall clip) and
  `pinball-tilt-cross.mp4`, for every question. `pinball-tilt-mid.mp4` and `pinball-tilt-sweep.mp4` ran
  for Q3's table only, unguarded and unfiltered, and are descriptive. Hashes pinned, fatal; the target is
  `pinball.wnft`, `9e8eb486…`.
- **Seeds 1 to 8, policy `frame`,** through `runSeeding`. A backend without `deterministicRansac` is
  refused, and `countGlobalRandomReads` must read zero on every run. Both fatal.
- **Schedules:** every frame, and the 15 and 25 ms device schedules at `DEVICE_ACQUIRE_MS` (static 36.9 ms,
  table 41.0, wall 20.4) and the borrowed 36.9 ms on the tilt clips, detection 83.2 ms. The scaled step is
  left out, as #246 left it out: its timing does not repeat. **The 15 and 25 ms schedules are one reading on
  the static, table and tilt clips** (their rows print the same, as in #228's records); they differ on the
  wall clip.
- **Loops: 6 a run, loop 0 warm-up, loops 1 to 4 counted,** as #228's repeat ran them.
- **The frame loop** is `replayRun`, imported. The decoding and the device constants are copied from
  `scripts/replay-clips.mjs`, a top-level script, and proved by **check D**: at seed 1, every schedule, the
  static, table and wall clips' frames equal `replay-clips`' default exports (2 loops, a prefix, the stamp
  apart) and the tilt clips' equal its `--clips tilt` exports (6 loops, the stamp included), frame for
  frame, `trackerTimings` aside. Stdout lists each file's hash.
- **The runs, per clip, schedule and seed,** each stamped on the loops' continuous timeline unless named:
  A, the tracker at its defaults; G10 and G180, with `poseGuard` at 10° and 180° (campaign clips); F, with
  `poseFilter: { mode: "one-euro" }`, and FW, the same stamped `pts × 1000`, which wraps (static clip).
- **"The export"** is run A's frame records through `JSON.stringify` and `JSON.parse`, as an export carries
  them, with `processingResolution` and the target's size beside them. `K` is
  `intrinsics(processingResolution)`, checked equal to the replay's, element by element. The arms are derived
  from it alone: IPPE is its `poseCorners`; CF is `closedFormPose(H, K)` projected by the frozen `frameRecord`
  (its `poseCornersOf`); GUARD is CF where `tiltDegOf` of CF's `R` is below 10°, the export's `poseCorners`
  otherwise, as `guardPose` decides.
- **The metric** is the frozen `summarizeRun` on a whole run, and the frozen `cornerJitter` with its
  `include` option on a selection, the loops read from all of a run's counted frames, as `compareExports`
  reads one. The two agree on IPPE's whole run, bit for bit, on every run (fatal); and on the filter's.
- **Tilt** is the guard's: `acos(R[8])` of the closed form on the frame's own `H`, read on the control arm.
- **Q4's pairs** are one run's frames, or two runs proved to track identically, so their frames are common
  by construction. The paired difference is Y − X per seed. "Without" drops every occurrence, in every seed,
  of the single media time whose removal moves the median over the seeds of the difference the most.

## Q2: the filter's reading, and its clock

**Why `poseJitterPx` can move with the filter, from the code:** the tracker hands the filter's answer back
as the result's pose, `const pose = f.apply(r); return r.ok && pose !== r.pose ? { ...r, pose: pose! } : r;`
(`tracker.ts:968` to `969`); the frame record projects that pose,
`poseCorners: result.ok ? poseCornersOf(result.pose, K, targetPoints) : null`
(`examples/js/bench-frames.mjs:180`); and the summary reads them,
`const pose = cornerJitter(withPoseCorners(frames), clip);` (`examples/js/bench-summary.mjs:174`), with
`withPoseCorners` putting `poseCorners` in `corners` (line 125). The windows are of media time, not of
`timestampMs`.

**The reset rule,** quoted (`packages/nft-tracker/src/pose/pose_filter.ts:228` to `232`): "a timestamp
that does not exceed the previous frame's input timestamp resets, whatever that frame did, a reset frame
included … the frame after a reset seeds the filter with its own pose, unfiltered", in code
`const reset = pose === null || frame.state !== "TRACK" || !pose.good || !(ts > this.tLast);` (line 263).

- **Responsiveness:** the filter moves the reading by −0.0423 px at every frame and −0.0708 px on the device
  schedules, in every seed. These are #228's first record's −0.042 and −0.071, the same reading, now through
  the shipped `poseJitterPx`. The seeds are one observation on this clip: on the device schedules Q4 gives
  each arm's spread over them as 5.65e-14 px or less, with 6 distinct values of 8; at every frame, and for
  FW, stdout gives the readings to four decimals only, the same in all 8 seeds, and no spread.
- **The clock.** Under media-time stamps the filter resets at every wrap: 5 stamp steps back in a run of 6
  loops, each on a TRACK frame with a good pose, and F and FW part at the first one (seed 1: media 0.000 s,
  stamp 0.0 after 12133.3 at every frame, after 12100.0 on the device schedules). FW filters 10 frames fewer
  than F a run, the 5 resets and the 5 seeding frames after them. **The reading moves by −0.0322 px (every
  frame) and −0.0295 px (device), so the filter's effect reads 0.0745 rather than 0.0423, and 0.1003 rather
  than 0.0708.** Why a reset lowers the jitter is not measured here. A conjecture: the static clip's corners
  jump back at each loop (about 3 px, in the tracking mode's plan,
  `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-25-bench-nft-tracking-mode.md`, its D1), and a filter that carries across
  the jump spreads it over the next frames of the window, where a reset does not.
