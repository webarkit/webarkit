## Why this probe exists, in one paragraph

#104 refuses an ambiguous fit by its geometry, before the pose. #137's defect
is a detection's support: four correspondences, residual zero by construction.
#104's own probe measured that neither `d_scale` nor `d_orth` separates the
4-inlier detections from the rest (its item 3). #137's consequence and probe
item 8 together say round 1 of #104 cannot run as registered: it must run on
the hand-held clips, and there `jitterPx` cannot carry a counted comparison
while the gate stands. This probe settles one question only: **would fixing
#137's gate make that comparison possible?** It is read as two protocol
questions. Responsiveness: how much of the arm-to-arm difference in `jitterPx`
on a hand-held clip is carried by the detections the gate admits on 4
inliers? Expressibility: with those detections removed, what remains of the
draw's leverage?

## The method, and the checks that make the rest readable

- **The metric is imported, not reimplemented.** `cornerJitter`,
  `countedLoopFrames`, `isLoopWrap` and `mediaKey` come from
  `examples/js/bench-metrics.mjs`, unchanged. Every reading is
  `cornerJitter`'s own `include` argument; a non-DETECT frame is always kept.
- **Reproduction check, fatal, zero tolerance:** each run's whole-set
  `jitterPx` recomputed over its frames equals the recorded
  `runSummary.jitterPx` exactly, on 37 of 37 runs. It can fail; it did not.
- **Window-keying check, fatal:** the windows kept, counted by keying frames
  as `cornerJitter` keys them (`loop:floor(mediaTime)`) and applying its two
  eligibility rules, equal the module's `jitterWindows` under every reading
  of every run (148 of 148). It can fail; it did not.
- **Cross-check against the record:** the 20 M3 rows under the "none"
  reading, common media times, whole-set difference, common-set difference
  and frame-set part, equal the 20 rows the record's own script prints when
  run on the same day, to the printed precision, every one.
- **What a "4-inlier detection frame" is here.** A frame of state DETECT with
  `numInliers` 4: a frame posed by the detection's own fit on exactly four
  correspondences. The frame record's `numInliers` is `result.numInliers`:
  on a DETECT frame the detection's RANSAC inlier count, which #137's body
  reads it as; on a TRACK frame `tracking.inliers`, the count
  `DEFINITIONS.trackedPatches` describes, and the script confirmed the two
  equal on every TRACK frame. Only such a frame
  **with corners** can enter `jitterPx`, and `frameRecord` leaves `corners`
  null when the four corners do not fall on one side of the camera, so most
  4-inlier detections never enter it; the counts below give both.
- **The worker arm has nothing to exclude, and the script checks it.** Its
  consumed detections land as TRACK (the tracking step confirmed them on the
  consuming frame, so their corners are the tracking fit's and `numInliers`
  is the tracking count) or as LOST without corners: 0 DETECT frames in all
  six worker exports, 0 consumed frames in state DETECT. A consumed
  detection the tracking step refuses does leave its own inlier count on
  the LOST frame (reason `unconfirmed`, `trackLoss` `too-few-patches`): over
  the counted loops, 77, 66, 64 and 70 refused consumed detections with 4
  inliers on the wall runs and 30 and 44 on the table runs, none with
  corners. So the worker arm's **refused** 4-inlier detections are
  countable and enter nothing; whether a **confirmed** one began as a
  4-point fit cannot be read, since the TRACK frame carries the tracking
  count and `detection.jobs` carries no count at all (the reviewer's
  finding below corrected a first draft of this bullet, which said the
  worker arm's detections could not be classified by support at all). The
  exclusion is therefore one-sided on every M3 pair, and the common set
  under an exclusion is rebuilt from the reduced posed sets.
- **The four readings.** `none`: the record's figure. `X4`: 4-inlier
  detection frames excluded, **the question's reading**. `X6`: detection
  frames with 4 to 6 inliers excluded, the range #137's comment names
  ("detections of 4 to 6 inliers"); reported because the comment names it,
  not as a proposal. `XD`: every DETECT frame excluded, the ceiling of what
  any gate acting on detections alone could remove from a figure; a bound,
  not a design.
- **Known limitation,** as every derivation script's here: no test and no
  format check cover `docs/`, so the script could break silently later while
  its figures stay in the record, and a defect of its own would reproduce.
  Its guards are the two checks above; they catch a changed module or a
  changed export, not a defect of the script.

## What the 4-inlier detections are on the device, counted

Over the 37 runs: **3,920 DETECT frames; 1,246 of them 4-inlier detections;
77 of those carry corners; 2 of those 77 sit in a window that was eligible
(at least four frames with corners, nonzero time spread) before the
exclusion.** So the exclusion `X4` changes `jitterPx` in **2 of 37 runs**:
`tab9-m3-sync-wall-2` (88.68 → 5.20 px, one frame in a 6-frame window) and
`2026-09-26-tab9-ondevice-tracking-table-repeat` (4.34 → 2.84 px, one frame
in an 8-frame window). In the other 35 the excluded frames were the only
posed frame, or one of two or three, in their second of media time, and the
window was already outside the pool; the change is exactly zero.

That is the first structural difference from the seeded desktop replays
#137's comment and #104's probe read: there every frame is processed, every
window holds 25 to 30 frames and stays eligible, and a 4-inlier detection's
corners always land in the pool. On the device a sync-arm frame costs 45 ms
at the median and 128 ms at p95 on `sync-wall-1` (`summaryMs.total`), a lost
lock leaves a second of media time with one to three posed frames, and the
4-inlier detections are mostly those frames. This is a measurement of the committed exports, not a
claim about what a device run under a changed gate would show.

## Question 2: expressibility

reports the draw's leverage as `jitterPx` over eight seeds and its largest-
to-smallest ratio, on desktop replays. The committed exports hold no seed
sweep: every device run is unseeded, and the only repeated measurement of one
arm on one clip is its replicate runs within a session. So the figure is
taken in the comment's form over replicates:

> **R(reading) = max over the group's runs of whole-set `jitterPx` under the
> reading ÷ min over the same runs**, each run's `jitterPx` being
> `cornerJitter` over its frames (counted loops where recorded) with the
> reading's `include`. The range, max − min, beside it. A group is one arm,
> one session, one clip, two or more runs. The same on the media times every
> run of the group posed, which removes the frames the runs do not share.

## The refutation attempt on question 2's figure

The step protocol (lines 303 to 305): a figure that gates a decision gets one
independent attempt at refutation before it is recorded, and what the attempt
found is recorded beside it, including when it found nothing. Two attempts
were made: one built into the script, one by a reviewer with no part in the
derivation.

## What the committed exports could not answer, stated

- **The draw alone.** No committed device export is seeded; the replicate
  spread is the draw plus the device's frame schedule and state, and the
  exports cannot separate them (question 2 above).
- **The worker arm's confirmed detections' support.** A refused consumed
  detection leaves its inlier count on its LOST frame; a confirmed one
  leaves the tracking count on its TRACK frame, and the job records carry
  none. So whether any worker TRACK frame's lock began as a 4-point fit
  cannot be read from the exports. The exclusion is one-sided on every M3
  pair for that reason.
- **The `acquire-test` wall clips.** Their only committed exports predate
  corners; nothing on them is measured here.
- **A detection's own residual.** The exports carry no fit residual for a
  DETECT frame (#104's item 3 addendum), so a reading by residual rather
  than by count is not available post hoc.
