## Q0: does the pose feed back into tracking?

**No. Nothing but the result reads the pose. Every use, at `84fd4ba`, in
`packages/nft-tracker/src/tracker.ts` unless named:**

| Line | What reads it | What it does with it |
|---|---|---|
| 819–823 | `selected()` | runs `selectPose`, returns the selection, and counts a fallback (821) |
| 766–768 | `fallbackFrames` | the counter's only reader, a getter nobody in the tracker calls |
| 1090–1100 | `tracked()` | writes `pose` and `poseSelection` into the `"TRACK"` result, and nothing else |
| 1163–1174 | `detected()` | writes them into the `"DETECT"` result, and nothing else; `ok` here does not read `pose.good` |
| 872–873 | `process()` | hands the result to the filter, and replaces `pose` alone with its answer |

**What tracking does read:** the lock is written from `H` only (956, 997, 1064)
and cleared (912, 1012). `trackFrame` takes the two `H`s (1056–1063).
`tracking/*.ts` and `detection.ts` mention the pose in comments only: no line in
them reads `.pose`, `.R` or `.t` (`targetSide` reads `H`). `conditioning` reads
`H` and `K`, and is off by default. `replayRun`'s schedule reads `result.tracking`
and `result.state` (`replay-run.mjs:115`), and `frameRecord` reads `result.H`
(`bench-frames.mjs:145`).

**The filter's state** (`pose_filter.ts`, `apply`, lines 260 to 300) reads `ok`,
`state`, the unfiltered `pose` with its `good`, and `timestampMs`, and nothing else.
So it is a function of the pose sequence, the states and the timestamps. That is
what licenses a second tracker on the same frames.

**So the arms are one run's frames.** IPPE is `poseSelection.pose`; CF is
`closedFormPose(H, K)` on the same `H`; GUARD is CF below T and IPPE at T and above;
FILTER is a second `replayRun` with `poseFilter: { mode: "one-euro" }`, its omitted
parameters the registered `DEFAULT_POSE_FILTER_` values, asserted equal to the
registration's (1, 10, 1, 0.1, 1). **The identity check, fatal, exercised both
ways:** across the two trackers, per frame, `H` element by element, `ok`, `state`,
the reason, inliers, matches, `trackLoss`, the media time and the selected pose are
identical. With one element of one tracker's `H` moved by 1e-9, it fails.

**The stamp.** Both trackers run `unwrapped: true`: each frame's time on the loops'
continuous timeline, `--external` arm 1's convention and what the page's continuous
clock gives. Under `pts * 1000` the filter would reset at every wrap. The unfiltered
tracker only echoes the stamp, and `rngFor` is keyed on the media time. On the static
clip the filter carried across all 3 boundaries in the counted loops; on the
hand-held clips no boundary had a pose in the counted loops.

## Q1: the metric's definition, before any figure

- **The corners:** `targetCorners(w, h)` of the target's meta size, through
  `H' = K · [r₁ r₂ t]`, `K = intrinsics(w, h)` at the processing size, the same for
  every arm, with the frozen `reprojectCorners(H', corners)`. **No corners when
  `good` is false or any corner's depth is not positive:** `reprojectCorners` accepts
  all-negative `w`, which for a homography is the same homography, and for a pose is
  a target behind the camera. On this corpus that rule fired on no frame of any arm;
  only its constructed case exercises it.
- **The figure:** the frozen `cornerJitter` with its `jitterPx`, `spreadPx`,
  `posedFrames` and `jitterWindows`, imported from `examples/js/bench-metrics.mjs`,
  given the pose-projected corners where the export has its own. It needed no change.
- **Beside it, on the same frames:** the frozen reading under `H`, the export's own
  `corners`, equal to the replay's on every frame at seed 1 (check D).
- **The frames:** the counted loops (`countedLoopFrames`, loops 1 to 4 of 6). **The
  common set** is the counted frames on which `H` and every arm have corners. It is
  frame-level, since all arms are one run; `compareExports` on each pair is its
  cross-check, and agrees to 0.000000 px on every run. No arm lost a frame here, so
  the common set is every posed frame in the counted loops.
- **The bands:** 0–1°, 1–3°, 3–5°, 5–7°, 7–10°, 10–15°, over 15°, lower edge in, read
  from the control arm's tilt for every arm (Stage 2, its first rule). Filtering by
  band fragments the one-second windows, so each row carries its frames and windows
  (Stage 2, its third rule), and "n/a" means no window held four of its frames.
- **Beside the metric, labelled as not the metric:** the median over frames of the
  mean corner distance between two readings of one frame. It is there because of the
  responsiveness finding below: when jitter barely moves, it says whether the pose
  did.

## Q2: #133's guard, defined before any figure

- **The tilt source:** `acos(R[8])`, in degrees, of `closedFormPose(H, K)` on the
  frame's own `H`, the angle between the plane's normal and the optical axis. It is
  the dry run's `tiltOf` (`packages/nft-tracker/scripts/jitter-dry-run.mjs:189`) on the
  control arm's pose, and the characterisation's own ruling: "if ever adopted it reads
  the closed form's own per-frame tilt, never the previous frame's". `K` is assumed
  (`intrinsics`), so the tilt is the assumed camera's: the table's renders and the
  clips share the function, not a calibration.
- **The two rules, on the characterisation's table** (its extended Item 2, each row
  checked present in that file, fatal):

  | Rule | First grid tilt where it holds | Linear between 7° and 10°, not a threshold |
  |---|---|---|
  | The medians cross: median e(IPPE) ≤ median e(closed form); 0.178 > 0.110 at 7°, 0.101 ≤ 0.118 at 10° | **10°** | 9.40° on the difference, 9.43° on the ratio |
  | IPPE closer on at least half of the fits: 13 of 48 at 7°, 27 of 48 at 10° | **10°** | 9.36° |

- **The rule picked: the medians' crossing, read at its first grid row, T = 10°,** put
  to the advisor before any figure. Its reason: it is the rule #228's body names; it
  compares the two errors themselves; and a grid row is a measured value, where an
  interpolated point inside the interval is "a bound stated more tightly than it was
  measured" (the protocol). Between 7° and 10° the table does not resolve the crossing,
  and a guard exists to stop IPPE's loss, so the interval resolves toward CF. Since
  both rules read 10°, the other rule's threshold is the same; both interpolated values
  are reported below as frames handed over.
- **The sensitivity, T = 7°,** the interval's lower edge, where IPPE's loss is still
  measured: a per-frame choice on the same poses, no further tracker run.
- **The boundary:** IPPE at exactly T. Frames with no tilt keep IPPE; there were none.
- **Note the table's scale.** It is RMS error against a truth on rendered views. Nothing
  here measures that on the clips.

- **Inputs:** `pinball-static.mp4`, `pinball-bench.mp4`, `pinball-bench-table.mp4`,
  `examples/targets/pinball.wnft`, the tracker's defaults. Hashes are pinned, fatal.
- **Seeding:** `--seed-policy frame` semantics through `runSeeding(seed, "frame")`, seeds
  1 to 8. Each run refuses a backend that does not declare `deterministicRansac`, and
  `countGlobalRandomReads` must read zero. Both are fatal.
- **Schedules:** the every-frame run, and the fixed 15 and 25 ms device schedules at
  `DEVICE_ACQUIRE_MS` and `DEVICE_DETECT_MS`. No scaled step: it reads this machine's
  speed (#94, stop 3).
- **The 15 and 25 ms schedules are one reading on the static and table clips:** their rows
  hash the same: acquisition plus either step lands in the same frame period there
  (`replay-run.mjs:115`). They are two readings on the wall clip only. Tables below say so.
- **The run discipline (#151):** the script reads `git rev-parse HEAD`, `git diff HEAD` and
  `git status --short` at its start and end, fatal if they differ, and prints them. Both
  counted runs: `git diff HEAD` empty, status empty. No mutant or edit overlapped a run;
  the checks' defects are flags, not edits to the tree.
- **The wrong-pose rule, stated before any figure, read on the control arm:** a frame is
  flagged when a corner under `H`, or under CF's pose, lies more than one frame diagonal
  outside the frame. The diagonal is a stated bound, with no measured derivation. A pose
  with a non-positive depth has no corners and has already left the common set, since the
  corners are grid points; `inFront` false counted 0 for both CF and IPPE on every run. A
  second reading flags on any arm's corners. It selects on the treatment, so it is
  descriptive, and its figures are in stdout only.

**What the seeds and loops are, which every figure below rests on.** From `--post-run` (same
media time, state and `H` across loops 1 to 4 of a run):

- **On the hand-held clips the counted loops are copies.** At every frame, all eight seeds of
  both clips have one distinct loop in four. No pose reaches the loop boundary there (the first
  frames of each loop are `LOST`), so each loop starts from no lock and redraws the same
  detections under the same frame keys. On the wall clip at 25 ms two seeds have two distinct
  loops, and on the table clip at 15 and 25 ms three seeds do. So a figure on those clips rests
  on about one pass of the clip, and "36 windows" is 9 × 4.
- **On the static clip the lock carries across the wrap,** and the four loops differ.
- **The seeds are not replicates of the noise.** A seed reaches a TRACK frame only through the
  detection that started its chain. No TRACK frame has one `H` in all eight seeds, bit for bit,
  yet IPPE's jitter on TRACK frames spans 0.047 px over the seeds on the wall clip. On the static
  clip the 8 row sets hold 6 distinct ones, and their figures agree to six places. The seed range below
  is therefore the spread over the detection draws, not a noise estimate, and "of 8" counts in
  stdout are not eight observations. It is the range that is given here as the margin, and no
  tolerance may be derived from it.

## Responsiveness

**From the code.** The new reading changes one input of a frozen function. `cornerJitter`
(`examples/js/bench-summary.mjs`, at `84fd4ba`) reads `f.corners`, and per window:

```js
                let mean = 0;
                for (const f of w) mean += f.corners[c][axis];
                mean /= n;
                let slope = 0;
                for (const f of w)
                    slope += (f.mediaTimeSeconds - tMean) * (f.corners[c][axis] - mean);
                slope /= tSpread;
                for (const f of w) {
                    residuals +=
                        (f.corners[c][axis] - mean - slope * (f.mediaTimeSeconds - tMean)) ** 2;
                }
```

The corners under `H` (`bench-frames.mjs:145`) are not a function of the pose, by Q0, so `H`'s column is
identical in every arm. The corners through `K · [R | t]` are a function of nothing else.

**It moves under each treatment, on every clip**, in the tables above. A treatment that did not change a
frame's pose must not move it, and does not: the guard's jitter and spread equal CF's in every band below
10° and IPPE's in every band above, to the bit (fatal), and its difference from IPPE at 10° and above reads
0.000.

**What it cannot respond to. This is a finding about the metric, not a footnote.** The lines quoted fit a
straight line to each corner's x and y within each one-second window, and pool the residuals. So:

- **A constant offset between two arms reads as nothing.** IPPE and CF differ by 0.637 px of corner distance on the
  static clip, and by 2.8 px on the table clip; the jitter difference reflects none of the offset's mean. The self-test
  shows it directly: a constant offset, and straight motion, both read 0 on constructed corners, and a frame-to-frame
  alternation reads above 0.05.
- **A constant lag at a constant rate reads as nothing between two arms.** An arc itself reads, since the fit is a line:
  constructed, a corner 131.8 px out at 30°/s reads 1.3846 px, and the same arc 0.5° behind reads the same, difference
  below 0.000001 px, at each rate from 5 to 90°/s (`--post-run`). So constant-rate footage would show the rotation and
  hide the lag.
- **An attenuated motion reads as lower jitter.** Constructed, on an oscillation about the axis of 5° at 1 Hz: 5.2938 px,
  and at 0.8 of the amplitude 4.2358 px (`--post-run`). So on moving footage this metric cannot separate smoothing noise
  from smoothing signal, and lower is not always better, without a truth.

So where FILTER − IPPE is near zero on moving frames, that is no statement that the filter did nothing: it moved the pose
by a median 0.606 to 0.616 px on the wall clip while jitter moved 0.037 to 0.089, and without the one media time at 7.706 s
0.003 to 0.024. The sign in motion is what remains visible, and its cause is a conjecture: on the wall clip's 10–15° band it
is +0.145 to +0.185, which is what a filter bending a moving corner away from a straight line would give, and nothing here
measures that. By the protocol's wording rule, a zero here is void, not null.
