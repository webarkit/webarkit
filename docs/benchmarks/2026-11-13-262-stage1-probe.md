## The probe's design

### The arms

- **ORB:** the jsfeatNext backend as it is, on `examples/targets/pinball.wnft`
  (`9e8eb486…`).
- **TEBLID-256:** `teblidBackend(inner)`, the jsfeatNext backend with `describe` replaced and
  `capabilities.descriptors` `["teblid"]`, on `teblidTarget`: `pinball.wnft` with its one ORB set
  replaced by TEBLID-256 descriptors **of the same keypoints, in the same rows**, described on
  `examples/images/pinball.jpg` at compile-target's `maxSide` 640, which `pinball.wnft` records.
  The two arms differ by their descriptors only: keypoints, patches, pyramid and meta are the same
  objects. Its basis is checked before every run, fatally: ORB re-described by the same code on
  the same image and rows gives `pinball.wnft`'s stored bytes, 2,062 of 2,062 rows.
- **Why no change under `packages/` is needed, from the code at `d9092e4`.**
  `chooseDescriptorSet` (`packages/nft-tracker/src/detection.ts`) takes the first set whose
  `elementType` is `"bits"`, `norm` `"hamming"`, and `kind` in `cv.capabilities.descriptors`: the
  TEBLID set, under the wrapper's `["teblid"]`. `detectTarget` describes the scene with no `kind`
  (`cv.describe(frame, sceneKeypoints)`), which the wrapper answers with TEBLID. The backend's
  `match` refuses only differing `kind` or `norm` and reads `bytesPerDescriptor` from its inputs,
  32 for both. The target is not compiled by `buildTargetFromImage`, so its writer's set sorting
  does not arise.
- **The TEBLID call, exactly:** for each keypoint `k` (level-0 px; `k.level` its level in the
  backend's ∛2 pyramid; `k.angle` the backend's `orb.ic_angle`, radians, measured on that level),
  `new keypoint_t(k.x, k.y, k.score, k.level, k.angle)` with `size = 31 * Math.cbrt(2) ** k.level`,
  then `jsfeatNext.teblid.describe(img, kps, n, out, { bits: 256, scale_factor: 1.0 })`, `img` the
  full-resolution U8C1 frame. The scene is detected at one level (`DEFAULT_SCENE_LEVELS`), so every
  scene keypoint has `size` 31.

### The runs, and which figure is read on which

- **Tracking mode** (`arms`): `NftTracker` through `scripts/replay-run.mjs`'s `replayRun`, two
  loops, seeded `runSeeding(seed, "frame")`, seeds 1 to 8, tracker options `{ ratio }` only.
  Schedules: "every frame", and the replay's device schedules with a 15 and a 25 ms step, which
  use one detection cost, 83.2 ms, for both arms: a model of which frames a device processes, not
  of TEBLID's cost. **Read here:** lock rate (the share of frames in `TRACK`), first lock,
  re-acquisitions, first steps and their confirmations, held-lock steps lost, and lasting locks,
  through the page's own functions (`summarizeRun`, `reacquisitions`, `lockSteps`, with
  `clipDurationS` the clip's loop period). LOST frames are always counted as never yet acquired
  or lock lost, by the glossary's sequence rule.
- **Stateless mode** (`stateless`): `detectTarget` on every frame of one loop, no lock,
  `frameRng(seed, pts[i])`. **Read here, paired per frame:** matches, inliers, the share accepted,
  the reasons, and per-stage detection timings. In tracking mode the arms detect on different
  frames, since the treatment changes which frames hold a lock, so detection-level figures read
  there would compare different frame sets (Stage 2's common-frames rule). Timings follow a
  discarded warm-up pass per arm and are prototype timings: `describe` copies the frame into a
  matrix and allocates a keypoint object per keypoint.
- **What a seed varies.** Under the frame policy a seed changes RANSAC's draws only. Keypoints,
  descriptors and matches do not move with it, so ORB's seed spread is zero for matches by
  construction, and is stated for each figure before any comparison against it.
- **The cross-checks, fatal:** `check-orb` (the ORB arm's every-frame tracking run and its
  stateless run, at seed 1, frame for frame against this machine's R-replay exports), and the GMS
  mode's ratio-tested set against the stateless run, frame for frame.

### What stops the step, written before the arms

By Stage 1's rule, the step stops when the metric cannot respond or the corpus cannot express the
effect. Here, before any figure:

1. **Responsiveness.** TEBLID stops if, on both table and wall, none of lock rate, re-acquisitions,
   first steps confirmed and the stateless share accepted moves outside the ORB arm's range over
   the 8 seeds, on any schedule.
2. **Expressibility.** If the re-acquisitions and first steps per run are too few on every
   schedule for a difference to be told from one event (single digits on both clips), the corpus
   cannot express the effect, and the step is rescoped or deferred, not run.
3. **What the locks are worth.** TEBLID stops, or goes on only with a false-lock criterion, if its
   added locks are bought with wrong `H`s: more accepted detections than ORB on the mirrored
   frames (every acceptance there is wrong), or a larger share of wrong `H`s than ORB on the
   synthetic frames where acceptance is not saturated, or a first-step confirmation rate below
   ORB's range.
