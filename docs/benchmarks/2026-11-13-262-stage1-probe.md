## The probe's design

### The arms

- **ORB:** the jsfeatNext backend as it is, on `examples/targets/pinball.wnft`
  (`9e8eb486…`).
- **TEBLID-256:** `teblidBackend(inner)`, the jsfeatNext backend with `describe` replaced and
  `capabilities.descriptors` `["teblid"]`, on `teblidTarget`: `pinball.wnft` with its one ORB set
  replaced by TEBLID-256 descriptors **of the same keypoints, in the same rows**, described on
  `examples/images/pinball.jpg` at compile-target's `maxSide` 640, which `pinball.wnft` records.
  The two arms differ by their descriptors only: keypoints, patches, pyramid and meta are the same
  objects. Its basis is checked before every run, fatally: ORB re-described by the same code on
  the same image and rows gives `pinball.wnft`'s stored bytes, 2,062 of 2,062 rows.
- **Why no change under `packages/` is needed, from the code at `d9092e4`.**
  `chooseDescriptorSet` (`packages/nft-tracker/src/detection.ts`) takes the first set whose
  `elementType` is `"bits"`, `norm` `"hamming"`, and `kind` in `cv.capabilities.descriptors`: the
  TEBLID set, under the wrapper's `["teblid"]`. `detectTarget` describes the scene with no `kind`
  (`cv.describe(frame, sceneKeypoints)`), which the wrapper answers with TEBLID. The backend's
  `match` refuses only differing `kind` or `norm` and reads `bytesPerDescriptor` from its inputs,
  32 for both. The target is not compiled by `buildTargetFromImage`, so its writer's set sorting
  does not arise.
- **The TEBLID call, exactly:** for each keypoint `k` (level-0 px; `k.level` its level in the
  backend's ∛2 pyramid; `k.angle` the backend's `orb.ic_angle`, radians, measured on that level),
  `new keypoint_t(k.x, k.y, k.score, k.level, k.angle)` with `size = 31 * Math.cbrt(2) ** k.level`,
  then `jsfeatNext.teblid.describe(img, kps, n, out, { bits: 256, scale_factor: 1.0 })`, `img` the
  full-resolution U8C1 frame. The scene is detected at one level (`DEFAULT_SCENE_LEVELS`), so every
  scene keypoint has `size` 31.

### The runs, and which figure is read on which

- **Tracking mode** (`arms`): `NftTracker` through `scripts/replay-run.mjs`'s `replayRun`, two
  loops, seeded `runSeeding(seed, "frame")`, seeds 1 to 8, tracker options `{ ratio }` only.
  Schedules: "every frame", and the replay's device schedules with a 15 and a 25 ms step, which
  use one detection cost, 83.2 ms, for both arms: a model of which frames a device processes, not
  of TEBLID's cost. **Read here:** lock rate (the share of frames in `TRACK`), first lock,
  re-acquisitions, first steps and their confirmations, held-lock steps lost, and lasting locks,
  through the page's own functions (`summarizeRun`, `reacquisitions`, `lockSteps`, with
  `clipDurationS` the clip's loop period). LOST frames are always counted as never yet acquired
  or lock lost, by the glossary's sequence rule.
- **Stateless mode** (`stateless`): `detectTarget` on every frame of one loop, no lock,
  `frameRng(seed, pts[i])`. **Read here, paired per frame:** matches, inliers, the share accepted,
  the reasons, and per-stage detection timings. In tracking mode the arms detect on different
  frames, since the treatment changes which frames hold a lock, so detection-level figures read
  there would compare different frame sets (Stage 2's common-frames rule). Timings follow a
  discarded warm-up pass per arm and are prototype timings: `describe` copies the frame into a
  matrix and allocates a keypoint object per keypoint.
- **What a seed varies.** Under the frame policy a seed changes RANSAC's draws only. Keypoints,
  descriptors and matches do not move with it, so ORB's seed spread is zero for matches by
  construction, and is stated for each figure before any comparison against it.
- **The cross-checks, fatal:** `check-orb` (the ORB arm's every-frame tracking run and its
  stateless run, at seed 1, frame for frame against this machine's R-replay exports), and the GMS
  mode's ratio-tested set against the stateless run, frame for frame.

### What stops the step, written before the arms

By Stage 1's rule, the step stops when the metric cannot respond or the corpus cannot express the
effect. Here, before any figure:

1. **Responsiveness.** TEBLID stops if, on both table and wall, none of lock rate, re-acquisitions,
   first steps confirmed and the stateless share accepted moves outside the ORB arm's range over
   the 8 seeds, on any schedule.
2. **Expressibility.** If the re-acquisitions and first steps per run are too few on every
   schedule for a difference to be told from one event (single digits on both clips), the corpus
   cannot express the effect, and the step is rescoped or deferred, not run.
3. **What the locks are worth.** TEBLID stops, or goes on only with a false-lock criterion, if its
   added locks are bought with wrong `H`s: more accepted detections than ORB on the mirrored
   frames (every acceptance there is wrong), or a larger share of wrong `H`s than ORB on the
   synthetic frames where acceptance is not saturated, or a first-step confirmation rate below
   ORB's range.

## Question 1: can this repository score a detection's `H`?

**On synthetic frames, yes. On the clips the criteria run on, no.**

- **The source that can:** the ground truth's frames (`tracker_sequences.ts`, `frameAt`: the target
  fixture warped by a known `H`, blurred, noised, its exposure drifting). The score is
  `inView(H, T)`: the RMS distance, frame px, between where the detected `H` and the true `T` put
  the target's 64 patch centres, over the centres `T` puts inside the 270 × 360 frame. The scorer
  was exercised both ways, fatally: 0 on `T` itself; 3, 10 and 30 on `T` moved by 3, 10 and 30 px.
- **The source that cannot:** R-truth's capture is those same synthetic sequences; nothing in the
  repository holds a true `H` for any frame of the table or wall clips. A wrong detection on the
  clips cannot be told from a right one lost by its first tracking step.
- **On synthetic frames the two are told apart,** in the tracker's own sequence: on
  `leaveAndReturn` the target re-enters the frame, and 22 consecutive `DETECT` frames (70 to 91)
  each drop at their first step, with ORB at 21.70, 5.17 and 1.94 px for the first three and
  0.81 to 1.65 px for the rest; TEBLID at 4.92, 7.63, 2.73, then 0.67 to 1.80 px. So most of those
  first-step drops are **right detections lost by the step**, and the first two (ORB) or three
  (TEBLID) are beyond 2 px.
- **The tolerance, from the measured distribution:** accepted detections on the easy grid (the
  whole target in view, scale 0.30 to 0.60, any roll, mild perspective) score at most 2.16 px, 3,598
  of 3,600 at or below 2 px over both arms and five ratios. A right detection is therefore within
  about 2 px here. The hard grid (scale 0.12 to 0.25, strong perspective) shows a continuum from 2 to
  over 20 px, with no gap, so a single tolerance there is a choice, not a measurement: counts are
  given at 5 px and at 20 px.

| Grid, ratio 0.8 (8 seeds) | Arm | Accepted | ≤ 2 px | 2–5 | 5–20 | > 20 px |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| easy, 45 poses | ORB | 360 of 360 | 360 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

within about 2 px of the truth, and can count wrong acceptances on the mirrored clip frames. It
cannot tell a right lock from a wrong one on the table and wall clips themselves. Proposal for #268:
a false-lock criterion on the mirrored frames, the state the limit for the clips.

## The border and parity, for #264

From `2026-11-13-262-stage1-border-parity.mjs`
([output](./2026-11-13-262-stage1-analysis-border-parity.txt)):

- **The path rule,** read from the compiled `describe_all`: a keypoint takes OpenCV's fast path,
  with no bounds check, iff `x ≥ E`, `x + E < cols`, `y ≥ E`, `y + E < rows`, with
  `E = fround(fround(32 · fround(v / 64)) · 1.75)` and `v = fround(scale_factor · fround(size))`,
  that is `E = 0.875 · size` at `scale_factor` 1: 27.125 px at level 0 (size 31), 34.175 at level 1,
  43.058 at level 2. Otherwise the clamped path, whose box corners are clamped into the integral
  image (`Q`, `ye`). Both are OpenCV's, bit for bit.
- **20 px prevents every overrun, and not every truncation.** `BORDER = 20` applies on each level,
  so 20 · ∛2^level in level-0 px, below `E` = 27.125 · ∛2^level: every keypoint within
  7.125 · ∛2^level px of `BORDER`'s line takes the clamped path. The overrun the declaration
  describes, a box past the left or right edge reading the neighbouring row, is on the fast path
  only, which by its own rule never runs within `E` of an edge. **The patch's reach, measured**
  (part 3b: from a keypoint at x = 120, the smallest distance at which redrawing every column
  beyond it changes none of the 360 rolled descriptors): **22 px at level 0, 27 at level 1, 35 at
  level 2**, against `BORDER`'s 20, 25.198 and 31.748. So a keypoint on `BORDER`'s line has boxes the
  clamped path cuts by up to 2.0, 1.8 and 3.3 px. **The value TEBLID needs for no box to be cut is
  23 px at level 0** (28 and 36 at levels 1 and 2, measured); `E`, OpenCV's own fast-path margin,
  27.125 · ∛2^level, also suffices. **The wrap test** found no overrun: at the fast path's nearest admissible distance from the left and right edges, at levels 0,
  1 and 2 and every roll from 0° to 359°, 0 of 360 descriptors changed when the far side of the
  image was redrawn, and 0 of 360 at `BORDER`'s distance; its control, the patch's own columns
  redrawn, changed 360 of 360; the fast path at the top and bottom rows' distance threw in 0 of 2
  batches.
- **Where the keypoints fall:** the clamped path takes 2.09% of the scene's keypoints on table,
  0.72% on wall, 7.46% on static and 2.03% on cross (every frame, the tracker's detect options), and
  220 of `pinball.wnft`'s 2,062. Their descriptors are OpenCV's, with boxes truncated at the image
  edge.
- **For #264, so that ORB's keypoints do not change:** no change to `BORDER` or to `detect()` is
  needed for TEBLID to be OpenCV-exact and free of overruns; boxes cut at the edge are OpenCV's
  behaviour too. If #264 wants no truncated box in TEBLID's descriptors, the filter belongs in
  the TEBLID branch of `describe` or in the caller, never in `detect()`, which both families share;
  and since `describe` must return a row per keypoint (`buildTargetFromImage` refuses fewer rows),
  dropping keypoints there changes the contract's call pattern. This probe recommends neither.
- **Parity.** jsfeatNext 1.2.0's own OpenCV 4.10.0 fixture (`tests/fixtures/opencv/teblid-p256.json`
  at its tag 1.2.0, `9353b833…`, with its scene, as the border script's header pins them; the cases'
  names, borders and corners among them, are the fixture file's), run through the installed package: **681 of 681
  rows equal OpenCV's, byte for byte,** 20 cases, `scale_factor` 1 and 5, borders and corners
  included. **On our keypoints the comparison cannot be made:** the fixture's bytes are OpenCV's on
  the fixture's keypoints, and no OpenCV runs here. Two things #264 should know for a cross-check:
  `orb.ic_angle` returns radians in (−π, π], and 24.9% to 39.0% of our scene keypoints have a
  negative angle, which jsfeatNext treats as a rotation and OpenCV as upright; and OpenCV degrees
  enter through `Math.fround(deg) · 0.017453292519943295`, so a radian angle converted to degrees and
  back may not give the same float32 `cos` and `sin`.

## The verdict

**By the stop conditions written before the arms, TEBLID goes on to #264–#268, on two conditions.
The effect it goes on with is small, and the maintainer may rule otherwise.**

1. **Responsiveness: the figures respond** (stop condition 1 is not met), each of its four:
   - **lock rate:** at 0.85 TEBLID leaves ORB@0.8's range on table on every schedule and on wall
     every-frame, and ORB@0.85's on both clips every-frame; at 0.8 it moves at most 2 of 8 seeds
     above ORB's range on any schedule; on table's device schedules TEBLID@0.85 is above ORB@0.85's
     range in 1 of 8;
   - **re-acquisitions:** at 0.8 on wall every-frame TEBLID is above ORB's range in 4 of 8 seeds,
     and on wall device 25 ms in 5 of 8: more locks set again, not more frames tracked;
   - **first steps confirmed:** at 0.8 on wall every-frame 5 of 8 above; at 0.85 on table's device
     schedules 4 of 8 above;
   - **the stateless accepted share:** at 0.8 on wall 7 of 8 above ORB's range; on table 1 below;
     at 0.85 1 (table) and 4 (wall) of 8 above ORB@0.85's, the ranges overlapping.
2. **Expressibility: partly.** Wall expresses lock rate and event counts; table expresses lock rate
   only, its first steps per run being 2 to 16 (stop condition 2, applied per clip, which goes beyond
   its text; table's event counts are therefore not proposed as criteria). Static and cross cannot express a
   difference, as expected.
3. **What the locks are worth: the gain comes with wrong acceptances, so TEBLID goes on only with a
   false-lock criterion** (stop condition 3). On the mirrored frames TEBLID@0.85 accepts 3.2% to 3.5%
   of frames, every one wrong, against ORB@0.85's 2.4% to 2.9% and ORB@0.8's 0.2% to 1.0%. **And
   the first-step confirmation rate falls below ORB's range,** the condition's other trigger: at
   0.8 the share confirmed is below ORB's range in 2 of 8 seeds on wall every-frame and 1 of 8 on
   each wall device schedule, its mean lower on all three, while the count confirmed rises above
   ORB's range on wall every-frame (5 of 8). Both triggers lead to the same outcome under the
   condition: TEBLID goes on only with a false-lock criterion. The count of lasting locks is
   unchanged.

- **The size.** The gain at the proposed 0.85, table every-frame, is +0.045 in lock rate over
  ORB@0.8's mean (at 0.9, +0.051); on the device schedules it is +0.022 on table, where ORB@0.85
  gains +0.007 and TEBLID@0.85 is above ORB@0.85's range in 1 of 8, and on wall it does not leave
  ORB@0.8's range in one direction. The device's
  own run-to-run s in M9 is 0.021 to 0.022 on one seed. Whether a gain of that size justifies #264
  to #269 is a ruling, not a figure: if the maintainer stops TEBLID here, M10 closes on this probe,
  and what it leaves is that acquisition on table and wall fails on matching and on the first
  tracking step (M9's LOST reasons, re-derived above), which is #87/#89's ground, the tracking step,
  and that the right/wrong question on the clips needs ground truth, #109's ground.
- **GMS:** dropped as a question for M10. It adds no accepted detection where detection fails, and
  removes many where it succeeds.
- **False locks:** M10 can score a detection's `H` on synthetic frames, and count wrong acceptances
  on mirrored clip frames; it cannot tell a right lock from a wrong one on the table and wall clips
  themselves, and the registration states that limit.

  copies its decoding (`probe`, `greyFrames`), which it does not export. Nothing outside
  `docs/benchmarks/` changed.

## The maintainer's ruling (2026-11-14)

Read from the probe's own figures (`docs/benchmarks/2026-11-13-262-stage1-probe.md` and its analysis outputs):
- **At the same ratio the descriptor does not move the lock rate.** At 0.8, table every-frame: TEBLID 0.7963 against ORB 0.7917, 0 of 8 seeds outside ORB's range. The gain appears only at 0.85, a second change.
- **That ratio costs wrong acceptances.** On mirrored frames, TEBLID@0.85 accepts 3.46% (table) and 3.23% (wall), against ORB@0.8's 0.23% and 0.96%.
- **On the device schedules the gain is small or absent.** Table: +0.022 over ORB@0.8, and above ORB@0.85's range in 1 of 8 seeds. Wall: no consistent change. On wall device 15 ms, TEBLID@0.85 (0.7622) is below today's default, ORB@0.8 (0.7797).
- **The added locks do not last.** Lasting locks are equal between the arms in every run. On wall every-frame at 0.8, TEBLID sets about 11 more locks a run and about 2 of them confirm.
So the failure the probe localises is **the first tracking step after a detection**, not the descriptor. TEBLID gives more matches and more inliers. The tracker cannot use them while the locks they set drop at their first step.
 
**The condition to reopen:** re-run this probe's scripts after a change that raises the share of first steps confirmed after a detection. If, at the same ratio as ORB, TEBLID's lock rate or its first steps confirmed then leave ORB's range on the device schedules, #264–#268 are reopened. If they do not, TEBLID is dropped for this tracker, in a second dated entry.
